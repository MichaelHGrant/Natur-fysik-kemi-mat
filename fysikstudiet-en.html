University physics – my 1978 Sussex B.Sc. revisited and brought up to date

In 1978 I graduated with a B.Sc. (Hons) in physics from the University of Sussex at Brighton. Almost half a century later I have tried to recreate the courses – not as exam notes, but as they would look today: with the history behind them, the central equations, interactive figures you can experiment with yourself, and what has happened in each subject since 1978. Every page can also be downloaded as a PDF.

Sussex in the 1970s

The University of Sussex was founded in 1961 as one of Britain’s new “plate glass” universities, with buildings by Basil Spence in the park at Falmer outside Brighton. Physics was organised in the School of Mathematical and Physical Sciences, and teaching was built on the British tutorial system: lectures supplemented by weekly tutorials in small groups, where you handed in problems and had to defend your solutions to a researcher.

It was Anthony (Tony) Leggett who interviewed me and offered me my place at Sussex, and he was my tutor in elementary particle physics. He received the Nobel Prize in 2003 for his theory of superfluid helium-3 – work he was doing at Sussex in exactly those years – and he died on 8 March 2026. Among my other teachers was Colin Finn. In a way, the pages below are a greeting to them.

The courses are arranged here by subject rather than by year of study. The first twelve pages, and the pages on mathematics, electronics and the Minoans, were written for this collection; the others are earlier pages on the site that cover the rest of a physics degree.

Classical physics

Analytical mechanics

Lagrange, Hamilton and the principle of least action – mechanics rewritten so that it points towards quantum mechanics.

Lagrange · Hamilton · Least action · Newton’s Principia

Fluid mechanics

Bernoulli, Euler and Navier–Stokes, the Reynolds number, Prandtl’s boundary layer, lift and circulation, water waves and turbulence.

PDF · Wind tunnel · Towing tank

Since 1978: computer simulation (CFD) has taken over much of the wind tunnel’s work; whether Navier–Stokes always has smooth solutions became one of the Clay Institute’s Millennium Prize Problems in 2000.

Continuum mechanics

The stress tensor, Mohr’s circle, elastic moduli, P and S waves, Euler’s buckling load, fracture and viscoelasticity.

PDF · Earthquakes

Since 1978: finite-element calculations on a PC have become the engineer’s standard tool; auxetic materials with negative Poisson’s ratio (1987) and 3D-printed metamaterials.

Electromagnetism

From Coulomb and Ørsted to Faraday and Maxwell’s equations: field lines, electromagnetic waves, the Poynting vector, resonant circuits and fields in matter.

PDF · The spectrum

Since 1978: metamaterials with negative refractive index (2000); in the 2019 SI reform the elementary charge became exact – and $\mu_0$ a measured quantity.

Heat and matter

Thermodynamics

The laws, cycles and Carnot, entropy, free energies and the Maxwell relations, phase transitions.

PDF

Since 1978: the fluctuation theorems for small systems (Jarzynski 1997, Crooks 1999); Landauer’s principle on the heat cost of information has been measured experimentally (2012).

Statistical mechanics

Boltzmann and Gibbs, the partition function, Maxwell–Boltzmann, Bose–Einstein and Fermi–Dirac, the Ising model and critical phenomena.

PDF

Since 1978: Wilson’s renormalisation group (Nobel 1982); Bose–Einstein condensation in atomic gases (1995); Parisi’s theory of spin glasses (Nobel 2021).

Properties of matter

Forces between molecules, van der Waals and critical points, surface tension, viscosity, elasticity and transport.

PDF · Cornflour and water

Since 1978: “soft matter” became a field (de Gennes, Nobel 1991); quasicrystals (Shechtman 1982, Nobel 2011).

Solid state physics

Crystal lattices and X-ray diffraction, phonons, free electrons, band theory, semiconductors and magnetism.

PDF

Since 1978: the quantum Hall effect (1980), high-temperature superconductors (1986), graphene (2004), topological insulators – and the blue LED (Nobel 2014).

Plasma physics

Debye length and plasma frequency, particle orbits and drifts, magnetohydrodynamics, waves and fusion.

PDF · Aurora

Since 1978: JET ran from 1983 to 2023 and set the record of 69 MJ of fusion energy; NIF achieved ignition in 2022; ITER is under construction.

Superconductivity and superfluidity

Kamerlingh Onnes, Meissner, London, BCS theory, the Josephson effect, helium-4 and helium-3.

PDF

Since 1978: Leggett’s Nobel Prize (2003) for superfluid helium-3; copper oxides (1986), iron-based superconductors (2008) and hydrides under extreme pressure.

Quantum physics and light

Quantum mechanics

From Planck and Bohr to Schrödinger and Heisenberg: wave functions, potential wells, tunnelling, spin, the hydrogen atom, entanglement and Bell’s inequality.

PDF

Since 1978: Aspect’s Bell experiments (1982), the loophole-free Bell test (2015) and the Nobel Prize of 2022; quantum computers and quantum cryptography have become engineering.

Atomic physics

Spectral lines and Bohr’s model, hydrogen orbitals, fine structure and spin, the Zeeman effect, the periodic table, Moseley and atomic clocks.

PDF · Spectra

Since 1978: laser cooling and Bose–Einstein condensates (1995), optical atomic clocks with 18-digit precision, trapped ions and Rydberg atoms as quantum computers.

Optics

Fermat’s principle, refraction and the rainbow, matrix optics, interference, N-slit diffraction, Fresnel’s equations and resolving power.

PDF · Lenses · Fourier optics

Since 1978: adaptive optics, Hubble (1990), super-resolution microscopy (Nobel 2014) and the Event Horizon Telescope’s image of a black hole (2019).

Laser physics

Stimulated emission, population inversion, rate equations, resonators and modes, Gaussian beams and pulses.

PDF · The laser

Since 1978: laser cooling (Nobel 1997), frequency combs (2005), optical tweezers and amplified ultrashort pulses (2018), attosecond pulses (2023).

Nuclei, particles and relativity

Nuclear physics

The structure of the nucleus, binding energy and the liquid-drop model, the shell model, radioactivity, fission and fusion.

PDF

Since 1978: the periodic table is complete up to element 118; the neutron-star merger of 2017 showed where gold is made.

Particle physics and group theory

Symmetries and groups, SU(2) and SU(3), the quark model, the Standard Model and the Higgs mechanism.

PDF · String theory

Since 1978: the W and Z bosons (1983), the top quark (1995), neutrino masses (1998) and the Higgs boson (2012).

Astronomy and astrophysics

Celestial mechanics, stellar spectra and distances, the HR diagram, the life and death of stars, galaxies, cosmology and exoplanets.

Not a course: supplementary reading for the related courses – and a lifelong hobby.

PDF · The solar system

Since 1978: dark energy (1998), more than 6000 exoplanets since 1995, Gaia, James Webb, gravitational waves and images of black holes.

Relativity

Special and general relativity: the Lorentz transformation, spacetime, the equivalence principle, curvature and black holes.

PDF

Since 1978: GPS corrects for relativistic effects every day; the accelerating expansion of the universe (1998), gravitational waves (2015) and the image of a black hole (2019).

Mathematics and electronics

Mathematics and Further Mathematics

The preliminary courses: analysis and Taylor series, linear algebra and eigenvalues, Fourier and Laplace transforms, integral equations.

PDF

Since 1978: the FFT in every phone, wavelets, and linear algebra on the scale of PageRank and neural networks.

Calculus III

Functions of several variables, gradient and directional derivative, Lagrange multipliers, multiple integrals, divergence and curl, the theorems of Gauss and Stokes.

PDF

Since 1978: automatic differentiation computes gradients in millions of dimensions – the basis of training neural networks.

Applied differential equations

Oscillations and resonance, the phase plane, stability and limit cycles, special functions, partial differential equations and Fourier series.

PDF

Since 1978: chaos theory became a field in its own right; epidemic models became everyday news during the covid-19 pandemic.

Complex variable theory

Euler’s formula, analytic functions and Cauchy–Riemann, conformal maps, Cauchy’s theorem, Laurent series and residues.

PDF

Since 1978: the Mandelbrot set (1980) and domain colouring on colour screens; the Riemann hypothesis is still unproved.

Numerical mathematics

Rounding errors, bisection and Newton–Raphson, interpolation, numerical integration, linear systems, Euler, Runge–Kutta and symplectic methods.

PDF

Since 1978: IEEE 754 (1985), LAPACK and adaptive solvers; graphics processors made parallel computing cheap.

Electronics I and II

Diodes and rectifiers, transistors and amplifiers, field-effect transistors, feedback and operational amplifiers, oscillators and noise.

PDF · Circuit theory · Digital electronics

Since 1978: from a few thousand to tens of billions of transistors on a chip; the microcontroller made electronics programmable.

Arts/Science

Archaeology of Ancient Crete – the Minoans

Evans and Knossos, the palaces, Linear A and Linear B, the Thera eruption, the decline – and radiocarbon dating, tree rings and DNA.

PDF

Since 1978: ancient DNA (2017) showed where the Minoans came from; in 2025 the Minoan palaces became World Heritage.

What has happened since 1978?

When I took my degree, the Standard Model was just becoming accepted, the charm quark was four years old, and the bottom quark had been discovered the year before. Pocket calculators were new, and “computer” meant a room full of punched cards. The most striking thing on re-reading the courses is not that the foundations have changed – Maxwell, Boltzmann and Schrödinger stand where they stood – but how much of what were thought experiments then is laboratory work today: single atoms trapped with light, entangled photons over hundreds of kilometres, gravitational waves from colliding black holes. At the same time some of the big questions are the same: turbulence, the interpretation of quantum mechanics, the relationship between quantum theory and gravity, and what dark matter and dark energy are. Some even speak of a crisis in physics – see Is physics in crisis?

None of the pages replaces a textbook; each ends with suggestions for further reading. They are meant as an overview for school students thinking of studying physics, for students who want to see how it all fits together, and for old physicists who – like me – want to see where the subject has got to.
