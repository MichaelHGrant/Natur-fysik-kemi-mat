The physics behind the phenomenon

When cognac "dances" in the glass, it is due to an interplay between adhesion, evaporation and surface tension – driven by the Marangoni effect.

  • The gradient arises: Alcohol evaporates faster than water. As the alcohol evaporates from the thin film on the glass, the concentration of water increases, and so the surface tension becomes higher here than at the bottom of the glass.
  • The liquid is pulled upwards: Liquids are pulled towards regions of higher surface tension. This upward force pulls more liquid up from the bottom.

The Role of Temperature (the Catalyst)

Warming the cognac glass in your hand is not just for the sake of cosiness; it changes the physics dramatically:

  • Increased evaporation: Heat makes the alcohol evaporate faster. This creates a steeper concentration gradient, which pulls the liquid up the glass more aggressively.
  • Thermal Marangoni effect: Evaporation requires energy, which cools the film locally. Since lower temperatures usually mean higher surface tension, this thermal drop works together with the loss of alcohol to pull the liquid up.
  • Lower viscosity: The warm liquid becomes more fluid. There is less resistance as the film climbs up, and when the tears finally fall quickly back down under the influence of gravity.

The mathematical description

The change in surface tension $\gamma$ depends on both the alcohol concentration $c$ and the temperature $T$:

$$ d\gamma = \left(\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial c}\right)_T dc + \left(\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial T}\right)_c dT $$

The Marangoni stress (the force that pulls the liquid upwards) is given by the gradient of the surface tension ($x$ is the distance up the glass):

$$ \tau = \frac{d\gamma}{dx} = \frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial c} \frac{dc}{dx} + \frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial T} \frac{dT}{dx} $$

Because adding alcohol lowers the surface tension ($\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial c} < 0$), and the concentration falls up the glass ($\frac{dc}{dx} < 0$), the first term becomes positive. At the same time, higher temperatures often lower the surface tension ($\frac{\partial \gamma}{\partial T} < 0$), and evaporation makes the temperature fall up the glass ($\frac{dT}{dx} < 0$), which makes the second term positive. The net force ($\tau > 0$) pulls the liquid up until gravity takes over.
