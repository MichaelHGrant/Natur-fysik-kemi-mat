What is perfect contrition?

Perfect contrition (contritio perfecta) is the sorrow for sin that springs from love of God himself — because sin wounds Him who is infinitely good and loves us — and not merely from fear of punishment. The latter is called imperfect contrition, attrition, and is in itself a good beginning, but it is love that makes contrition perfect.

According to Catholic teaching (Trent, and the Catechism of the Catholic Church §1451–1452), perfect contrition, joined with the sincere resolution to go to confession as soon as possible, can already now blot out even mortal sin. The Church still expects one to seek the sacrament of confession when the opportunity arises — contrition does not replace confession, but precedes it.

Angerakt

Full form

O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended Thee,
and I detest all my sins because I dread the loss of heaven and the pains of hell,
but most of all because they offend Thee, my God,
who art all good and deserving of all my love.
I firmly resolve, with the help of Thy grace,
to confess my sins, to do penance
and to amend my life. Amen.

Short form

O my God, I am sorry with all my heart for my sins,
because they have offended Thee, who art worthy of all love.
With the help of Thy grace I will confess, do penance
and live better. Amen.

Pray it slowly, preferably on your knees, and let each word rest — especially the sentence saying that it is love of God, not fear, that drives contrition.
