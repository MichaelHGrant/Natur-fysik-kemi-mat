§ 01 — The problem with nothing

Removing everything is harder than it sounds.

In general relativity, space and time are interwoven into one fabric — spacetime. You cannot remove one without the other. And even if you could, quantum field theory would protest: "empty" space bubbles with virtual particles that are constantly born and vanish.

Philosophers criticised Lawrence Krauss's book A Universe from Nothing precisely because his "nothing" was still a quantum vacuum — a fabric of fields, laws and mathematics. A true nothing would be something physicists cannot describe, for all description presupposes something.

§ 03 — Emergent spacetime

Spacetime may not be fundamental.

Spin networks: geometry from pure relations

Several modern candidates for a theory of quantum gravity suggest that spacetime emerges — it is not a stage but a consequence. Space itself consists of something more fundamental, and what that is depends on whom you ask.

§ 04 — Lisa Randall & the hidden dimensions

If spacetime exists, perhaps it is
warped.

While some physicists try to make spacetime disappear altogether, Lisa Randall — a theoretical physicist at Harvard — has taken another route: what if there are more dimensions than we see, and spacetime is drastically curved in a way that hides them?

Randall–Sundrum: our brane, their brane, warped 5D space in between

The Randall–Sundrum model (1999)

Together with Raman Sundrum, Randall proposed that our universe is a 4-dimensional "brane" embedded in a 5-dimensional space. The extra dimension is warped — curved in such a way that it can be infinitely large without our noticing it.

Hierarkiproblemet

Why is gravity so staggeringly weak compared with the other forces? Randall's model offers an answer: gravity "lives" on another brane and is strong there. What reaches us is only a diluted remnant that has travelled through the warped extra dimension.

Why it matters for "nothingness"

Randall's work shifts the question: perhaps it is not removing spacetime that reveals the bedrock of the universe — perhaps it is discovering that spacetime has more dimensions that we simply cannot see. What we call "empty space" may be densely populated — just not here.

Randall's point: spacetime with extra dimensions can be so warped that even an infinite extra dimension can exist without our noticing it. — freely after Randall and Sundrum (1999)
§ 05 — What lies underneath?

Perhaps reality,
at the deepest level,
is information.

The deeper we dig, the less the deepest level resembles things in a space, and the more it resembles relations between something we can barely name. Bits. Correlations. A mathematical pattern that "is" nowhere, because "places" only arise on top of it.

A universe without space, mass or time may not be an absence. It is the foundation.
