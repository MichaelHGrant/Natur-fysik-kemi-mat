الحمراء

ALHAMBRA

»The red fortress« — a palace built of light, water and infinite geometry.

GRANADA · ANDALUSIA · 1238–1492

WALK THROUGH THE PALACE
PROLOG

A dream in stonethe story begins at the gate

On top of the Sabika hill in Granada the Alhambra rises like a revelation — reddish walls of clay, lime and brick under the Andalusian sun, surrounded by cypresses and snow-capped mountains. For just over 250 years the Nasrid dynasty ruled here as the last Muslim kingdom in western Europe.

Behind the sharp fortress walls another world awaits: courtyards where the water runs quietly, ceilings of honeycomb cells and walls covered with inscriptions. »There is no victor but Allah« — the phrase is repeated more than eight thousand times in the palace stucco.

»Give him alms, woman, for there is nothing in life as cruel as being blind in Granada.« — FRANCISCO A. DE ICAZA
ROOM I · THE COURTYARD
بهو الريحان

Patio de los ArrayanesThe Court of the Myrtles

You step into a space that almost holds its breath. Before you: a 34-metre-long pool, edged with dark green myrtle hedges, which mirrors the mighty Comares Tower so perfectly that the sky seems to lie beneath your feet.

The water is not mere decoration — it is architecture. It enlarges the space, doubles the colonnades and creates a silence so dense that you whisper without knowing why. Here the sultan received his guests, wrapped in the sound of fountains and the scent of myrtle.

ROOM II · THE THRONE ROOM
قاعة السفراء

Salón de los EmbajadoresThe Hall of the Ambassadors

The ceiling above you represents the universe. 8,017 pieces of cedar wood, assembled in seven levels — the seven Islamic heavens — meet in a star that represents the throne of Allah. It is not decoration. It is theology.

Here, in 11×11 metres, Sultan Yusuf I sat in his throne niche and received envoys from all over the Mediterranean. In January 1492 Boabdil surrendered Granada to the Catholic Monarchs. According to legend, the last Muslim ruler wept as he left the city, and his mother said drily: »Do not weep like a woman for what you could not defend like a man.«

ROOM III · THE INNER PALACE
بهو الأسود

Patio de los LeonesThe Court of the Lions

In the heart of the Alhambra twelve marble lions stand in a circle carrying a fountain on their backs. According to a persistent legend the fountain once worked as a water clock, with a new lion spouting water every hour — but this is not documented. The water itself is driven by gravity alone from channels higher up.

Around the courtyard stand 124 slender marble columns. The stucco of the ceilings hangs like stalactites, and the floor is divided by four water channels — a rendering of the four rivers of paradise described in the Qur'an.

ROOM IV · THE GREEN ESCAPE
جنة العريف

Generalife»The Architect's Garden«

When the sultan wanted to escape the court ceremonies, he crossed a small bridge to the Generalife — the summer residence and gardens, where he could become simply a human being again. Here there are no throne rooms. Here there are only cypresses, winding paths, fountains and rose beds.

The water becomes music: in the Patio de la Acequia a long channel runs down the middle, and from each side fine jets spurt in an arc, so that they meet like slender crystal bridges over your footsteps. The jets, though, are a 19th-century addition; the garden itself is medieval and among the oldest surviving gardens in Europe.

From the Nasrids to UNESCO

Six hundred years on one timeline — from hill fortress to world heritage.

1238

The foundation

Muhammad I ibn al-Ahmar founds the Nasrid kingdom and moves his court to the Sabika hill above Granada.

1333–1391

The golden age

Yusuf I and Muhammad V build the Comares Palace, the Court of the Lions and the richly ornamented stucco and tilework.

1492

The fall of Granada

Boabdil surrenders the city to Ferdinand and Isabella; the Reconquista is complete and the palace becomes a royal residence.

1526

Charles V's palace

The Renaissance palace rises in the midst of the Islamic complex — an unusual meeting of two worlds.

1812

Destruction

Napoleon's troops blow up several towers; the palace decays and at times shelters squatters.

1832

Washington Irving

Tales of the Alhambra awaken worldwide interest and lead to the first restorations.

1984

UNESCO World Heritage

The Alhambra, Generalife and Albaicín are inscribed — today Spain's most visited monument.

The Language of Pattern

There are exactly 17 mathematical symmetry groups for a plane, repeating pattern — this was first proved in 1891. Many mathematicians believe they can find all of them in the Alhambra (others count fewer), and the craftsmen were certainly exploring them in practice as early as the 14th century. M.C. Escher copied the patterns in 1922 and again in 1936 — they became the seed of his own tilings. Try the symmetries yourself in the workshop below.
